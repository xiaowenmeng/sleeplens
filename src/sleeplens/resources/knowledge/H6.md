# NIH/NHLBI · Sleep phases and stages

## NREM

Non-REM sleep includes N1, the transition from wakefulness; N2, established sleep; and N3, deep or slow-wave sleep. Clinical staging uses brain activity measured in sleep studies. Light/deep labels in a consumer app must be checked against that product's definitions.

## REM

REM means rapid eye movement sleep. Brain activity resembles waking activity, dreaming commonly occurs, and muscles normally relax. REM and non-REM recur through the night; REM is not a synonym for deep sleep.

## Pattern

Deep sleep tends to occur more early in the night, while REM tends to increase later. Brief awakenings can occur between cycles. Sleep patterns change with age. This educational page supplies no universal required deep-sleep or REM percentage and does not interpret a person's tracker totals as a diagnosis.
