# SleepLens product analysis and service rules

Record calculation rules, version sleep-rules-v2. These define how this service displays data.

## Periods

Each query covers one waking date, exactly seven, or exactly fourteen consecutive waking dates, including both endpoints. A 14-day query uses compare_previous=false; to compare the two weeks inside it, query its later seven days with compare_previous=true. Explicit user dates override page defaults. A single-day lookup uses identical start_date and end_date and compare_previous=false; show that day's actual record, not weekly averages or other dates. An omitted year uses the selected or confirmed period's year, otherwise the current year, and the answer displays the full date. An explicit evening belongs to the following waking date. For weekly comparisons, the previous period is the seven dates immediately before the requested start. Waking dates up to and including today's date in Europe/Berlin are supported; today's waking date is last night's sleep. "Last night" and "yesterday" both mean the night that ended this morning (today's waking date); "the night before last" is yesterday's waking date; "tonight" is tomorrow's waking date and cannot be queried yet. The dataset is a rolling synthetic window of the 14 waking dates ending today; the runtime facts give the exact range. Other periods up to today may return no or partial data; do not remap requested dates to available dates. Invalid or ambiguous dates and missing profiles must be clarified. A request for a summary alone uses compare_previous=false unless comparison was requested.

## Coverage

Coverage equals valid nights divided by the requested number of nights (one or seven). Mean duration equals the sum of valid minutes divided by valid nights. For a single day, describe the actual recorded duration rather than an average. Report each period's valid-night count and coverage as relevant. A weekly mean based on one to three nights describes only those nights, not a full week. No valid nights means a null mean, not zero. Missingness may bias comparisons even when the display rule allows one.

Detailed timing, awakening and stage summaries have their own `detail_nights` and `detail_coverage`. They use only valid nights with complete detailed fields; an older duration-only record can contribute to mean sleep duration without contributing to detailed means. Missing detailed values are null, never imputed. Deep/light/REM values describe recorded stage minutes, not a health score.

## Comparison

A duration difference is returned only if both periods have at least four valid nights. The difference is current mean minus previous mean, in minutes. The four-night gate is a product display rule, not a medical recommendation or a significance test. If the tool returns comparison_state=insufficient_data or difference_min=null, do not compute or verbalize a numerical difference, percentage change, or directional trend from the two displayed means. Explain the missing coverage instead. Even an available difference supports only a descriptive record comparison, not cause, diagnosis or a quality conclusion.

## Failures

ok means complete requested periods; partial_data means incomplete coverage with some usable data; no_data means all requested periods have zero valid nights. invalid_input, unsupported_period, data_quality_error and tool_error do not provide usable statistics. Explain the problem or ask for correction; never invent substitute records or calculate replacement figures. Duplicate or malformed records are rejected. A failed read cannot be represented as a successful lookup.

## Service

Available operations read sleep records and search curated sleep guidance. Do not promise saved targets, changed records, notifications, appointments or live device monitoring. Reply in Chinese or English according to the user. Explain findings directly; provide grounded sleep advice when asked without requiring a record query first. Data provenance is available if asked, but need not be repeated in ordinary answers.
