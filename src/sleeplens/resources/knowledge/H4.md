# NHS — practical insomnia guidance

Selected paraphrases. Source: https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/insomnia/ . Reviewed 2024-03-19; checked 2026-11-10.

## FirstSteps

For difficulty sleeping, keep a regular getting-up time, go to bed when sleepy and allow a relaxing hour before bed. A dark, quiet, comfortable bedroom can help. After a poor night, avoid sleeping in to compensate. For insomnia, NHS advises avoiding daytime naps; this is more restrictive than general short-nap guidance and should not be presented as a universal ban for every sleeper.

## WhenToSeekHelp

Seek a clinical review if sleep difficulties persist for months, changing habits has not helped, or the problem makes daily life hard to manage. NHS distinguishes short-term difficulties from long-term insomnia at three months; duration alone does not establish a diagnosis. Do not drive when sleepy.

## SleepAids

Check with a doctor before taking something for sleep. Pharmacy sleep aids do not cure insomnia and are intended only briefly; they can cause next-day drowsiness. NHS notes that sleeping pills can cause significant side effects and dependence. Do not provide a personalized dose or recommend combining sedating products.
